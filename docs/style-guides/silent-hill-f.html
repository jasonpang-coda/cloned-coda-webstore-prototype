SILENT HILL f

Find the beauty in terror. A psychological horror set in fog-bound 1960s rural Japan — period elegance curdling, surface by surface, into bodily dread.

Source: konami.com/games/silenthill/f/eu/en · Konami Digital Entertainment / NeoBards Entertainment. Reference-derived fan style guide — not an official brand document.

01 · Brand Essence

ELEGANT · UNSETTLING
FOLKLORIC · VISCERAL · RESTRAINED

SILENT HILL f transplants the series' psychological horror to a mountain-pass town, Ebisugaoka, built on a stated visual thesis: "the beauty in terror, and terror from beauty." Every surface — hand-painted key art, a weathered brush logotype, flesh-and-flower monster design — negotiates between period-Japanese refinement and creeping bodily horror.

Elegant [observed]

Painterly matte key art; a single Mincho serif typeface carries every line of copy on the site; delicate red-spider-lily and hydrangea floral framing.

Unsettling [observed]

Fog-choked, empty village streets; the protagonist's hollow, restrained stare; new characters revealed with a blurred face until their card unlocks.

Folkloric [observed]

A masked fox-spirit guide in haori/hakama; a Shinto-shrine family among the cast; higanbana (spider lily, Japan's "flower of death") as key art centrepiece.

Visceral [observed]

Flesh-and-blossom body-horror monster design (Oi-omoi); saturated blood-red "otherworld" interiors in the gallery, sharply breaking the film's muted palette.

Restrained [observed]

"Cheerful and energetic as a child, she's grown to become much more restrained, and now hardly smiles" — the cast bios describe the tone the whole site enacts: muted teal-grey environments, no exclamation points in marketing copy, content-warning language that asks players to "take a break" rather than pushing through.

01·B · Brand Voice & Tone observed · live site copy

How SILENT HILL f Speaks

Literary, restrained, third-person myth-telling for the story itself, opening onto warm first-person testimony from cast and developers in the credits. The player is almost never addressed directly as "you" — an unusually indirect stance for a game storefront.

Voice pillars

Thesis-driven

"A story of impossible decisions, of the beauty in terror, and terror from beauty."

Restrained escalation

"An average teen, she passes the time with nothing particularly special about her life. Until, that is, a deep fog descends on Ebisugaoka…"

Binary-fate framing

"Will she choose to embrace elegance and beauty? Or will her path lead her to madness and horror…"

Duty-of-care voice

"If you feel uncomfortable at any point while playing, please take a break from playing or speak to someone you trust."

Warm, humble cast voice

"I almost felt my own sanity slipping at times, but it was all worth it…" — Konatsu Kato, actress for Hinako.

Under-stated commerce voice

"SILENT HILL f Celebrates Over Two Million Copies Sold Worldwide" — a sales milestone framed as an accolade, footnoted like a citation, not a banner shout.

Lexicon — say / avoid

✓ Brand vocabulary
  • fog, beauty, terror, struggle, decision, elegance, madness, partner, fate
  • "psychological horror" (not "survival horror" or "action horror")
  • third-person names: Hinako, Shu, Ebisugaoka
✗ Off-brand
  • hype exclamations ("AMAZING!", "don't miss out!")
  • gamer-bro slang, meme-speak
  • discount/urgency commerce copy ("SALE", "LIMITED TIME")

Webstore copy examples (in-voice)

02 · Logo & Clear Space

Logo

"SILENT HILL" is set in distressed, hand-brushed white capitals with torn, scratched edges. The lowercase "f" is oversized, italic, and rendered as a fine brushstroke in a single vivid crimson (#C90800) — a signature mark set apart from the mechanical capitals, reading almost like a wound or a stroke of blood across the wordmark. This lockup is a locked raster/vector asset, not a live webfont composition — reproduce it as an image, never retype it.

Reference asset reproduced at reduced resolution for internal design-reference use only. © Konami Digital Entertainment.

Reference asset: logo_silenthill_f.png · white wordmark sampled #FFFFFF · "f" sampled #C90800 (dominant fill, observed pixel sample)

✓ Approved usage
  • White wordmark + crimson "f" on black or dark photographic backgrounds only (the only colourway observed)
  • Keep clear space ≥ the height of the capital "S" on all sides
  • Keep the "f" in its exact crimson — never recolour it to match a secondary palette
✗ Misuse — never do this
  • Don't recolour the white capitals or the red "f"
  • Don't separate the "f" from "SILENT HILL" or resize it independently
  • Don't clean up, smooth, or outline the distressed edges
  • Don't place on busy light backgrounds without a dark scrim
  • Don't reset the wordmark in a different typeface

Contrast & placement rules

  • White/off-white (#EEEEEE) on Void Black (#000000) ≈ 19:1 — the default body/heading pairing everywhere on the site.
  • Champagne Gold (#E0CA7E) on Void Black ≈ 12.6:1 — safe for headings, labels, and body copy; this is the brand's real workhorse accent.
  • Fog Sage (#7A9A9C) on Void Black ≈ 6.5:1 — safe for body copy.
  • Blood Red (#C90800) on Void Black ≈ 3.3:1 — passes only for large/display text (≥24px) or non-text accents; never set body copy in red-on-black.
  • White on Blood Red ≈ 6.0:1 — safe: this is how the brand actually uses red (as a badge/CTA fill under white text), never as small text on black.
  • Never place body copy directly on the saturated crimson key-art regions — the site's own gold/white text always sits on a darkened photographic scrim, not flat colour.

The emitter.ts only injects ref seeds + optional palette ramps + font + page background — everything else resolves automatically. Figma token exports drop the --x- prefix; the repo adds it.

06 · Typography

Type System System Constant Font Brand-Locked

The live site actually splits into two font roles, confirmed by reading common.css directly: headings (.cmn-page-title, .cmn-section-title — the "Story" / "Characters" / "Monster" / "Gallery" labels) are set in font-family: yu-mincho-pr6n [observed: CSS rule] — Yu Mincho Pr6N, a real Morisawa/Adobe Fonts Japanese Mincho typeface served via Typekit (kit fia5mgy). Body/UI text (EU/English locale) falls back to "Times New Roman", "vdl-v7mincho", "source-han-serif-kr-subset", "source-han-serif-cn-subset", serif [observed: CSS rule on body] — VDL V7 Mincho rarely renders for Latin copy since Times New Roman satisfies it first; it exists to catch any embedded Japanese glyphs. Neither family is on Google Fonts. Proxy for reproduction: Noto Serif JP for both roles — the real family names stay first in any CSS stack. The only other type surface is the bespoke brush logotype, which is image-only (see Logo page).

Tier 1 — Type Primitives (Mobile / Desktop)

07 · Component Style DNA

Visual Building Blocks

Shape language Brand-Locked

--x-sys-radius-0 throughout — every UI surface (buttons, panels, thumbnails, nav) is sharp-cornered. Softness lives only in imagery (fog, flowers, monster silhouettes), never in chrome.

Lighting model Brand-Locked

Interactive buttons carry a photographic brass bevel implying one soft top light; non-interactive chrome is flat matte black. Key art is diffuse and overcast, except "otherworld" rooms which bloom with a single warm internal source.

Effect tints Brand-Locked

--x-sys-effect-shadow-primary: #322E21 (bronze, under brass buttons) · --x-sys-effect-glow-primary: #C90800 (crimson, for danger/otherworld emphasis) · hover: brass darkens toward bronze · pressed: bronze deepens further [inferred — no live hover state observed].

Density & spacing Brand-Locked

Airy — full-viewport section breaks with generous vertical whitespace. Base gap maps to --x-sys-space-xl. Grouping is space-grouped, not border-grouped: sections are separated by breathing room, not dividing lines.

Elevation Brand-Locked

--x-sys-elevation-flat everywhere — content sits directly on black or full-bleed photography. No card shadows observed; any "lift" is implied only by the photographic bevel on purchase buttons.

Stroke weight Brand-Locked

--x-sys-stroke-thin gold hairline under section headings (observed, e.g. under "Story"). Button borders are a photographic bronze frame, not a flat stroke — treat as a signature surface (see Tier-2).

Material & texture Brand-Locked

Heavy, defining. Paper-grain key art, scratched-brass button textures, a distressed logotype — texture is core brand identity here, the opposite of "clean flat."

Motion language Brand-Locked

Slow scroll-reveal: sections fade + translate up on entry, ~500ms ease-out [observed]. No snappy/bouncy micro-interactions anywhere — motion is as measured as the copy.

Iconography Brand-Locked

Minimal to none. Platform marks (PS5/Xbox/Steam) and a single red "!" content-warning flag on locked gallery thumbnails are the only icon-like elements observed.

Semantic colour mapping Store-Flexible

Default: bg-action-primary → sys-colour-primary-main. Stores may map to any intensity step (subtle through inverse).

Interactive vs non-interactive rule

Interactive controls (Buy Now, platform links, nav) carry a photographic brass/bronze frame with an implied top-lit bevel; everything else is flat matte black or full-bleed image with zero elevation and zero overlay.

08 · Art Direction & VFX

Imagery System

Framing & subject

Bust-to-waist ¾ portraits, camera near eye height, subject often off-centre with negative space filled by symbolic foliage (spider lily / hydrangea).

Background

Environmental and painterly — matte-painted floral walls or foggy village exteriors, not solid or abstract fields.

Lighting & mood

Diffuse, overcast, cool; a single warm interior source appears only inside "otherworld" gore rooms, breaking the cool mood deliberately.

VFX signature

Volumetric fog/haze, fine film grain, no lens flare or particle sparkle — restraint is the effect.

Figure-ground

Environmental depth, not flat cut-outs — characters are composited into the painted scene, not isolated on solid colour.

Text-over-art

Wordmark sits on the darkest region of the frame with no separate scrim box; supporting copy uses a soft vignette darken rather than a hard panel.

✓ Do
  • Let fog and foliage carry compositional weight instead of graphic UI chrome
  • Keep lighting diffuse/overcast except for isolated "otherworld" accents
  • Reserve saturated red for a single focal shock, not a wash
✗ Don't
  • Don't add lens flares, sparkle particles, or hard rim light — off-brand
  • Don't flatten characters onto solid colour cut-outs
  • Don't saturate the whole frame in red — it only works as contrast
